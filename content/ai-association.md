I am an officer of the BYU AI Association and lead its weekly AI in Research meeting, a
group working out how researchers in different fields can use these tools without handing
over the judgment that makes the work theirs. I wrote the proposal for the group, which is
public as the AI-In-Research repository.

What I bring to it is mostly evidence rather than opinion. I built a clinical imaging
application with an AI coding agent in weeks instead of months, and I am running a
controlled test in the Jensen Lab of where an agent helps and where it gets a pipeline
wrong. Both sides of that show up in the meetings.

I am also honest about the cost. These tools make research faster, and they change how
people think, not only what they produce. As more writing and reasoning gets handed over,
the average output gets more capable while the individual gets less practiced: less nuance,
less argument, less of the slow work understanding is made of. Holding the acceleration and
the cost at the same time is why I would rather help shape how AI enters science than just
use it.
