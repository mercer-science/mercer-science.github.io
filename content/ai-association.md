I am an officer of the BYU AI Association and lead its weekly AI in Research meetings. The group explores how researchers across disciplines can use AI tools effectively without giving up the judgment and responsibility that make the work their own.

The group is expanding its work across campus. I recently presented our initiative to a BYU Healthcare Leadership course and will be presenting to faculty in the College of Physical and Mathematical Sciences. Our officers are also beginning to meet with research labs to understand their needs and develop AI tools tailored to the problems their principal investigators want to solve.

I also want our group to consider the costs of adopting AI. These tools can make research faster and expand what individuals can accomplish, but relying on them too heavily may weaken the writing, reasoning, and problem-solving skills that develop through doing the work ourselves. I believe researchers need to understand both the capabilities and limitations of AI so they can use it to advance science without losing the skills that make good research possible.
