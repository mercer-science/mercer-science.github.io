### Problem

The Summer Premedical Research Internship needed a program mark. My first design was a DNA
double helix unzipping into a Y, with serpent heads at the tips: Y for BYU, DNA for
research, serpents for medicine. Three ideas in one shape.

### Solution

Viewers told me it read as female reproductive anatomy, with the serpent heads reading as
sperm. That is not a matter of taste; it is structural. Three cues stack: a smooth ovoid
with a single eye-dot at the end of a curving stroke is how sperm is drawn in a textbook,
the arms spread wider than they rise, and a narrowing coiled column descends from the
junction. Any one is harmless. Together they draw the whole diagram, and fixing any two
collapses the reading.

So I rebuilt the mark on an axis model rather than as a fixed set of drawings. Form, flare,
arm path, head shape, stroke weight, rung treatment, and whether the fork reads as a
replication fork each became a dimension you can set independently, which made combinations
reachable that the old list of thirteen finished drawings had no button for. Narrowing the
flare cuts the spread from 92 units to 64 and flips the rise-to-spread ratio from 0.83 to
1.41, which is the difference between a fallopian tube and a letter Y.

### Result

An interactive kit with four presets, built as HTML so anyone can click through the axes
rather than request a revision. It went to BYU Media. The lesson I keep: when feedback says
a design reads wrong, the useful move is to find which cues produce the reading and break
enough of them, instead of defending the intent.
