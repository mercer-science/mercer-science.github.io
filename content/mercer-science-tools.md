I recently began publishing research tools through the Mercer Science organization on GitHub. While several projects are still under development and remain private, I have begun making tools publicly available as they become ready for broader use.

**paper-engine** is my primary public project: a collection of skills, scripts, and workflows designed to streamline manuscript development, from an initial research idea to a submission-ready draft. It addresses the often tedious but important work of keeping outlines, figures, statistical results, and references consistent as a manuscript evolves. I developed it from firsthand experience at UT Southwestern, where I worked through more than eight revisions of a research manuscript and identified opportunities to automate much of the process.

**github-ai-project-manager** is a project in development that uses skills and hooks to enable AI command-line agents to manage research projects through GitHub. Its goal is to keep documentation, progress tracking, and records of decisions and changes synchronized with the actual work.

**AI-In-Research** contains the founding proposal and organizational framework for the BYU AI Association group I lead, which explores practical applications of AI in research.

I plan to continue expanding Mercer Science with tools that extend beyond my own workflows and can benefit other researchers. Several additional projects are in development, including a redesigned spaced-repetition platform inspired by Anki, a popular flashcard application among medical students.

The repositories are available at [github.com/mercer-science](https://github.com/mercer-science).
