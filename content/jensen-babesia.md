### Question

*Babesia duncani* invades red blood cells using an apical complex, a machine at one end of
the parasite built from membranes and cytoskeletal parts. What it looks like in three
dimensions is unknown, and no cryo-electron tomogram exists for any species in the genus.
A 2026 paper described a new tubulin rosette at the apical end of a sister species using
light microscopy, which gives a defined structure to test at much higher resolution.

### Approach

These cells are too thick to image whole, so the workflow needs cryo-focused ion beam
milling to thin them, which means running samples at the University of Utah. No milling
workflow exists for any *Babesia*, so part of the project is building one. I wrote the
plan and Dr. Jensen approved it. This is the riskier of my two projects and the one with
the most competition, which is why it runs second.

### Status

**Ongoing: in biosafety approval.** Nothing ordered, nothing cultured, no grids and no
milling runs yet. The plan and the project directory exist; bench work waits on the
biosafety committee.
