I am drawn to the problems that emerge between scientific discovery and practical application. In chemistry, I learned to build and troubleshoot research instruments. In biology, I became interested in using cryo-ET to understand molecular machinery inside cells. At UT Southwestern, I developed software to make research imaging workflows faster and more accessible to clinical teams.

Across these experiences, I have found myself working at the intersection of disciplines, using software and AI to help researchers do things that were previously difficult or impractical. I also lead the BYU AI Association's AI in Research meetings and have participated in discussions about data sovereignty and responsible AI development.

That is why I am applying for the Wild Card position. My interests do not necessarily fit neatly into one discipline, and I see that not as a lack of direction, but as an opportunity to connect ideas and approaches that might otherwise remain separate. I want to bring that perspective to problems that have not yet found a conventional solution.
