What I have actually done at the bench, as opposed to read about.

**Cryo-EM sample preparation and imaging.** Thermo Fisher Scientific's Resolution Evolution
course, October 2026, with hands-on time on the Vitrobot for plunge-freezing and on the
Krios cryo-transmission electron microscope.

**Human apoferritin expression, Jensen Lab bootcamp, September 2026.** I grew *E. coli*
BL21 Star (DE3) carrying a human ferritin H expression plasmid, prepared LB and 2×YT media,
and ran overnight starters, induction, and a plasmid miniprep on the lab's protocol. The
culture steps are what I have done so far; purification and grid preparation come later.

**Scanning electron microscopy.** Trained on a Thermo Fisher G4 Verios, with time on a
Helios and in immersion mode, imaging thin films and measuring their thickness in ImageJ.
I taught two newer students to do both.

**Ultra-high-vacuum and surface analysis.** Leak checking, bakeouts, and flange work on a
chamber running near 10⁻¹⁰ Torr, with X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy and mass
spectrometry on the same system.

**Coursework with real lab components.** Advanced Biochemistry Methods 1 (CHEM 584, a
master's-level course, in progress), Biochemistry Methods (CHEM 384), Molecular and
Cellular Biology Lab (MMBIO 241), and Advanced Physiology Lab (CELL 363). Genetics
(PWS 340), Molecular Biology (MMBIO 240), and Mechanisms of Molecular Biology (CHEM 482)
are the lecture courses behind them. Structural Biochemistry (CHEM 489) and Advanced
Biochemistry Methods 2 (CHEM 586) come in Winter 2027.
