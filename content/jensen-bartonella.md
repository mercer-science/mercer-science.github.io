### Question

*Bartonella henselae* carries a dense layer of adhesin on its surface, and a published
hypothesis holds that this layer physically blocks the secretion system the bacterium uses
to inject proteins into host cells. That idea rests on phenotypes alone. Nobody has
imaged the surface to see whether it is true, and no tomogram of any species in the genus
exists.

### Approach

Cryo-electron tomography freezes cells without stain or fixation and images them from many
angles, which gives a three-dimensional view of the surface at molecular resolution. These
cells run 0.3 to 0.5 µm across, small enough to plunge-freeze and image directly on BYU's
Krios without milling them thinner first, and they double in about three hours, so the
cultures move quickly. I scoped and wrote the project plan, Dr. Jensen approved it, and I
run the bench work. The known risk is that adhesin and secretion-system expression fade
with passage, so expression has to be checked right before freezing rather than assumed. A
follow-on project looks at the gene transfer agent particle from the same cultures.

### Status

**Ongoing: in biosafety approval.** The project directory, plan, and workflow are written.
Nothing has been cultured and no grids have been frozen, so there are no images or data
yet. Work starts once the institutional biosafety committee approves the protocol, which
is the order Dr. Jensen requires.
