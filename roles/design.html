Design

Designing tools for people whose work I actually understand.

I am not a design major, and I will say that plainly. What I offer is design grounded in the problem: interfaces for clinicians and researchers, designed around their decisions, and built by the same person who understands the science and can write the code. My strongest case study is the diffusion MRI application I rebuilt at UT Southwestern.

A Case Study in Designing for Real Users

Who it is for

Physicians and medical physicists who needed to see a patient's imaging and decide where a tumor was still active, without wrestling with the software to get there.

What was wrong

The tool they inherited ran one patient at a time, one step at a time, every step clicked by hand. There was no way to look at the results, compare across days, or get a figure out without going back to the raw files.

What changed

I designed a set of focused views on top of the processing: an image viewer that lines up anatomy and maps day by day, a comparison view, a figure creator that previews quickly and then renders at full quality, a tool that outlines treatment-resistant tissue and exports it straight to the planning system, and a fit checker a physicist can click through voxel by voxel. The before-and-after and the fit checker are shown under Projects. I shared drafts with the physicians as web previews so they could react to a working interface rather than a description, and the design changed in response to what they said.

Visual Communication

  • Figures. I build reproducible, automated figure pipelines so a paper's figures regenerate from the raw data, and I designed a flow-chart figure for a manuscript using a layout tool driven by AI.
  • Posters and slides. I have designed posters and presentation decks for conferences in chemistry, medicine, and structural biology.
  • Identity. I designed an interactive program logo for a summer research fellowship, which was sent on to the university's media team.

Why This Combination Is Useful

A designer who has to ask what the data means slows a scientific team down. I can sit with a physician, understand the clinical decision, sketch an interface, and then build it. That loop, from user to design to working software, is the part I am good at, and it is where design makes the most difference in research.
